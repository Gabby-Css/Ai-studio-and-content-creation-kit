2 · The brand

Promise

“Every song is a trip — and everyone is welcome aboard.”

KiddoVerse is a pocket-sized galaxy of little worlds. A crew of seven friends hops on the Zoomie (a rocket-bus) and visits a new Pocket World in every song, learning something and feeling something on the way.

Personality (3 words)

BrightKindCurious

Voice: warm, playful, never sarcastic, never scary. Short sentences. Sing-along-able. A grown-up should enjoy it on the 20th replay.

Global, not local

The brand is deliberately placeless: a pocket galaxy, a mixed-heritage crew, kid-universal themes (colours, counting, feelings, shapes, friendship). Culture appears as guest worlds — e.g. “Jollof Jamboree” (West Africa), “Lantern Lane” (East Asia), “Samba Sky” (Brazil), “Taco Twirl” (Mexico), “Bhangra Bazaar” (South Asia) — one-off episodes where the crew visits and the music/colours change. That lets African/Nigerian stories shine naturally without defining the whole brand.

Content pillars

🎵 Zoomie Songs
3-min music videos. Learning goal baked into every verse.
💛 Feelings with Nimbus
1–2 min emotional-skills songs (the “weather” idea).
🔧 Gizmo’s Lab
Numbers, shapes, tiny science, 1–2 min.
💃 Kiddo Wiggle
15–40 s dance challenges — the viral engine.
📖 Pocket Stories
5–8 min stories, a moral, a surprise.
🌙 Moon Library
Sleepy lullaby versions, 10 min loops.

Always / Never

Always:
  • Original music, characters and words
  • One clear learning idea per song
  • A move kids can copy (clap, stomp, wiggle)
  • Kind endings; every character gets a moment
Never:
  • Imitate another brand’s characters, logo, melody or catch-phrases
  • Scary / violent / mean-spirited humour
  • Fast strobing flashes or screaming audio
  • Real children’s faces or voices without consent

Where we deliberately went further

  • Retention re-hooks every 8–16 s: a new world, prop, move or sound arrives constantly (dance break, freeze gag, hatching eggs).
  • Participation moments: “Wiggle left! Wiggle right! Jump! Spin! Clap! Stomp! FREEZE!” — kids do something, so they watch to the end (and parents save it).
  • Fan-building: seven distinct characters → favourites → merchandise, books, apps.
  • Sonic branding: the 3-note motif and the “Kiddo-verse!” chant.

Not copied — on purpose

No characters, song, melody, lyrics, logo, colour-caption identity, setting or catch-phrase from the reference. Original music (synthesised from code), original lyrics, original characters and a new logo. This is both the legal-safe and the brand-safe approach: an imitation can never become the name kids ask for.

7 · Platform playbooks

▶ YouTube (long-form + Shorts)

  • Set “Made for kids = Yes” on every upload (required for kids’ content). It limits comments, notifications and personalised ads — so growth must come from packaging, playlists and watch-time, not community posts.
  • Title = hook + keywords + brand. Thumbnail = one big face + one big word, 3 colours (see thumbnails in the kit).
  • Upload the SRT; add chapters; put the learning outcomes in line 1–2 of the description (parents search for them).
  • Playlists: “Zoomie Songs”, “Learn with KiddoVerse”, “Dance & Wiggle”, “Feelings with Nimbus”. End screen → next song.
  • Cadence you can sustain: 1 song / week + 4–5 Shorts / week. Quality beats volume (see §9 on “mass-produced” content).
  • After 48 h open the retention graph: a cliff in the first 10 s = shorten the intro; a dip mid-song = cut or re-order that shot.

♪ TikTok

  • 9:16, 15–35 s, text hook on frame 1, burned-in captions, loop-friendly ending.
  • Use your own original sound and invite “use this sound” for the #KiddoWiggle challenge.
  • 1–2 posts/day for the first 30 days to learn what hooks work; keep the best 20%, remake them.
  • Reply to comments with a video (“Rumble counts to 5!”).
  • Mark AI-assisted content honestly (TikTok has an AI-generated-content label).

📷 Instagram Reels · Facebook Reels

  • Same cut-downs. Facebook is strong with parents 25–44 — make a Page, share into parent groups (read the rules first).
  • Instagram “Trial Reels” let you test a Reel with non-followers before pushing it to your followers.
  • Carousel posts: the 7 characters + a one-line catch-phrase (very shareable).

📌 Pinterest + 🎧 Streaming

  • Pinterest is slow-burn: colouring pages and flashcards link back to YouTube and keep working for years.
  • Distribute the song audio (DistroKid / TuneCore-type distributor) as a kids’ single — you own the masters because they were generated from your own code and words.

7 · The “different way” ideas (beyond posting)

1 · The Wiggle Challenge (UGC engine)

Give families one easy move per week. Parents post their own kids doing it with #KiddoWiggle. Rule: only share children’s videos with the parent’s explicit permission, never pull kids’ faces from comments, and never ask children themselves to post.

2 · “Where’s Bloop?” rewatch trick

Hide Bloop (or his antenna) somewhere in each video and say so in the description. Rewatching = watch-time. It also makes a great Short: “Did you spot him?”.

3 · Cliffhanger sparks

End every video on the silhouette of the next Pocket World. Next to it: “Which world is next?” poll on your parents’ page. Audience feels co-ownership.

4 · Parent value-add

Description starts with the learning outcome: “Colours, counting 1–5, feelings, shapes + a dance break.” Add a printable. Parents are the buyers; teachers are the amplifiers — share the SRT and sing-along version with classrooms.

5 · Seasonal drops

Original, non-copyrighted seasonal songs (rain day, birthday, new school, holiday-neutral winter, lantern night). Search spikes are predictable — release 3 weeks early.

6 · Guest worlds

Rotate cultures as visitable worlds (Jollof Jamboree, Samba Sky, Lantern Lane…). Work with a consultant / native speaker for each; credit them. It broadens the global audience and creates local-language search hooks.

9 · Safe & legal — what I checked, what you must still do

You asked me to avoid policy and legal problems. Here is the honest picture. I’m not a lawyer; where it matters, a trademark attorney should confirm.

① The name “KiddoVerse” — conflict found

  • US trademark KIDDOVERSE, owner 22LEARN, LLC, Reg. No. 5762831 (filed 2018, registered 28 May 2019) for downloadable computer game programs — Justia record.
  • Existing apps named Kiddoverse: Toddler Learning Games (Kiddoverse), kidSAFE-certified, KiddoVerse Kids & Toddler Game (App Store), KiddoVerse: Preschool Games (Google Play).
  • Why it matters: a video channel is a different category than game software, but your own plan includes apps and games — the same goods as the registration — and a near-identical kids’ learning brand invites a confusion claim, a takedown, or a forced rebrand after you have built an audience.
  • Options: (a) switch to a cleared name (cheapest now); (b) keep it as a channel/series name only and talk to a trademark attorney about coexistence; (c) use a distinctive modification and clear it. Because the name is one variable in my build, a rename = update BRAND, rebuild logo, re-render (≈45 min).

③ Platform policy 2026 — be careful

  • YouTube “Made for kids” / COPPA: required for kids’ content; comments & personalised ads are off and revenue is typically much lower (one industry report says by 50–80%) — TechTimes.
  • AI scrutiny is rising. In April 2026 200+ child-development groups asked YouTube to label or restrict AI-generated kids’ content (Fortune, Spokesman-Review). YouTube says it limits AI content on YouTube Kids to high-quality channels.
  • What this means for you: be transparent (“vocals are AI-synthesised” is already in the description), lead with original characters, human creative direction and real educational value, avoid mass-produced look-alike uploads, and plan to replace the synthetic vocals with a human singer as soon as budget allows — the single biggest trust and quality upgrade.
