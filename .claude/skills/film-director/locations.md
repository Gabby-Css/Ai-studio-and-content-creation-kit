# Locations — the five locks

A location is not a pretty frame. It is a **production contract**: actors can be blocked inside
it, the light has one logic, the camera can see depth, and a later view repeats the same
geography. Every downstream shot inherits these decisions, so slow down here.

Resolve all five before generating. If a rewrite drops one, **restore it before generating** —
a disappearing lock is the most common way a location silently becomes unusable.

| lock | the decision | why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| **GEOGRAPHY** | what is where, in thirds — "house right third, pens left" | screen-direction words like "on the left" are untestable later |
| **BLOCKING ANCHOR** | one named object to attach placement to — a sofa, a doorway, a street banner | "stands between the sofa's hall-side arm and the window" is testable in a later frame; "stands on the left" is not |
| **LIGHT LOGIC** | one source, named direction and quality; shadows fall away from it | two light logics = slop, instantly |
| **MATERIALS** | concrete nouns — "weathered wood siding", never "beautiful" | quality words give the model nothing to hold |
| **DEPTH / CONTINUITY** | atmospheric falloff + what must repeat in the reverse | haze is what tells the eye a background is far away |

---

## The two failures that are easy to skip past

1. **Oversharpened, oily surfaces.** No real texture to hold on to, so the surface swims and
   smears the instant anything moves across it. Fatal in video, invisible in a still thumbnail.
2. **No atmospheric haze.** Kill the falloff and near and far read at the same sharpness — flat,
   and immediately readable as slop. Haze IS depth.

## Passing checks, visible in one still
- One soft source, even tonality across every plane, nothing crushed, nothing blown.
- Or: one sun, consistent shadow direction on **every** tree and building, clean falloff into
  skyline haze.

**Neither proves a blocking route or reverse continuity.** A still can only prove light,
tonality, texture, and depth. Blocking and reverse are separate tests.

---

## Shoot 3/4, then test the reverse

**3/4 is the declared default.** It exposes side geometry and separates foreground, midground,
and background — which is exactly what gives later character placement visible floor and
distance cues.

Head-on flattens the location into a backdrop: characters stand *in front of* it, never *inside*
it. Worse, hidden side geometry stays unconstrained, so asking for an angle later gives the model
room to invent doors, walls, and object positions.

**The reverse is a separate generation and a continuity TEST.** Keep locked: the anchor, the
openings, the light side, the materials, the palette. Then **compare the two frames before
calling them the same location.** Confidence is not evidence.

---

## When one master wide is enough
When no cut depends on exact off-frame geography — some b-roll, montage, action coverage — one
wide plate can carry it. Mediums and close-ups will extend what the plate never showed, so use
this shortcut **only when that invention cannot break blocking or continuity.**

A montage passes when track, curbs, barriers, season, and palette hold. The same plate would
NOT prove an exact reverse or a repeatable turn.
